# Explanatory Notes

>### Milestone 1 (Before 1950): Philosophical roots of thinking machines

**What happened:** Myths like the Greek story of Talos, a bronze giant guarding Crete, and early mechanical automata showed people imagining artificial beings long before computers existed.

**Why it matters:** These stories raised the questions AI still wrestles with: what counts as thinking, and can it be built?

**Subfield:** Foundations

**Impact:** This groundwork shaped the ideas, ethics debates, and public imagination that later researchers drew on when AI became real engineering.

>### Milestone 2 (1950): Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"

**What happened:** Alan Turing proposed the Turing Test, which judges a machine by whether its behaviour is indistinguishable from a human's instead of debating whether it is conscious.

**Why it matters:** It replaced an unanswerable philosophical question with a practical, testable one, giving the field a measurable goal.

**Subfield:** Foundations

**Impact:** The idea still frames how we evaluate chatbots and assistants today, and it fuels debates about whether passing such a test means real understanding.

>### Milestone 3 (1956): The Dartmouth Summer Research Project

**What happened:** John McCarthy and colleagues gathered at Dartmouth, where McCarthy coined the term "Artificial Intelligence".

**Why it matters:** It gave the field a name, a community, and a shared research agenda, which is why 1956 is treated as AI's birth.

**Subfield:** Foundations

**Impact:** The researchers who attended went on to lead the major AI labs for decades, and the label they chose still defines an industry worth hundreds of billions of dollars.

>### Milestone 4 (1956): The Logic Theorist

**What happened:** Often called the first AI program, it proved mathematical theorems and even found a more elegant proof than the published one.

**Why it matters:** It showed that a machine could do something previously seen as pure human reasoning, not just calculation.

**Subfield:** Symbolic AI

**Impact:** It launched the symbolic approach to AI, where intelligence is built from logic and rules, influencing automated reasoning and theorem-proving tools still used in software verification.

>### Milestone 5 (1966): ELIZA

**What happened:** Joseph Weizenbaum built ELIZA, the first chatbot, which used simple pattern matching to mimic a therapist, yet users formed emotional attachments to it.

**Why it matters:** It revealed how readily people attribute understanding to machines that merely sound conversational, which is now called the "ELIZA effect".

**Subfield:** Natural Language Processing

**Impact:** It pioneered conversational interfaces and raised the ethical questions about trust and emotional reliance that today's AI companions and customer-service bots still face.

>### Milestone 6 (1980s): Expert systems flourish

**What happened:** Companies encoded specialist knowledge as rules, and R1 reportedly saved Digital Equipment Corporation $25 million a year by 1982.

**Why it matters:** It was the first time AI delivered clear commercial value, proving it could pay for itself in business.

**Subfield:** Symbolic AI

**Impact:** It sparked a wave of corporate AI investment, and its rule-based decision logic lives on in medical diagnosis aids, credit decisions, and business-rule engines.

>### Milestone 7 (1986): Practical learning for multi-layered neural networks

**What happened:** A practical learning algorithm for the multi-layered perceptron made it possible to train networks with hidden layers effectively.

**Why it matters:** It let networks learn from examples instead of being hand-programmed, solving problems earlier single-layer models couldn't.

**Subfield:** Neural Networks

**Impact:** It laid the technical groundwork for modern deep learning, which now powers speech recognition, image tagging, and translation.

>### Milestone 8 (1980s–1990s): The "AI Winters"

**What happened:** Funding and interest collapsed when AI's early promises went unfulfilled and expert systems proved costly and brittle.

**Why it matters:** It showed the danger of hype outrunning capability and taught the field to set realistic goals.

**Subfield:** Field History

**Impact:** The lesson still shapes how researchers and investors treat each new AI boom, and progress continued quietly in machine learning and statistics during these years.

>### Milestone 9 (1997): Deep Blue defeats Garry Kasparov

**What happened:** IBM's Deep Blue beat the reigning world chess champion, evaluating about 200 million positions per second.

**Why it matters:** It showed that machines could surpass the best humans at a task long seen as the pinnacle of intellect, through brute-force search.

**Subfield:** Game AI / Search

**Impact:** It brought AI into global headlines and proved that massive computation could rival human expertise, a theme that grew into later breakthroughs in games and planning.

>### Milestone 10 (2004–2007): The DARPA Grand and Urban Challenges

**What happened:** DARPA competitions pushed teams to build autonomous vehicles that crossed real desert terrain and later navigated a mock city.

**Why it matters:** They turned self-driving from a lab idea into a working engineering challenge, with open competition accelerating progress.

**Subfield:** Robotics / Machine Learning

**Impact:** Many participants went on to lead self-driving programs at major tech and car companies, shaping today's driver-assistance and autonomous-vehicle industry.

>### Milestone 11 (2006–2009): The Netflix Prize

**What happened:** Netflix offered a prize for improving its movie recommendations, drawing thousands of teams into applied machine-learning research.

**Why it matters:** It showed how open competitions with real data can speed up innovation and popularised techniques like ensemble methods.

**Subfield:** Machine Learning

**Impact:** Recommendation systems now drive what people watch, buy, and listen to across streaming, shopping, and social media.

>### Milestone 12 (2009): Fei-Fei Li introduces ImageNet

**What happened:** Fei-Fei Li publicly introduced ImageNet, a massive dataset of labelled images.

**Why it matters:** It supplied the large-scale data that learning algorithms needed, showing that data could be as important as the algorithm.

**Subfield:** Computer Vision / Data

**Impact:** The annual ImageNet competition became the proving ground for deep learning, and its success helped fuel the boom in image recognition used in phones, medicine, and security.

>### Milestone 13 (2011): IBM Watson wins Jeopardy!

**What happened:** Watson defeated champions on the TV quiz show, handling natural-language questions full of wordplay and ambiguity.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrated that a machine could interpret nuanced human language and retrieve answers from huge volumes of text in real time.

**Subfield:** Natural Language Processing

**Impact:** It pushed companies to apply language AI to fields like healthcare, finance, and customer support, paving the way for today's question-answering assistants.

>### Milestone 14 (2010s): The deep learning revolution

**What happened:** Large datasets combined with GPU hardware allowed Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to excel at image recognition.

**Why it matters:** It proved that neural networks, once sidelined, could beat traditional methods when given enough data and computing power.

**Subfield:** Deep Learning

**Impact:** Within a few years, deep learning powered face unlock, medical image analysis, photo search, and self-driving perception, and it became the dominant approach in AI.

>### Milestone 15 (2014): Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)

**What happened:** Researchers introduced GANs, in which two networks compete: one generates content and the other tries to spot fakes, pushing output toward realism.

**Why it matters:** It was a new way to make machines create rather than just classify, producing strikingly realistic images.

**Subfield:** Generative AI

**Impact:** GANs enabled AI-generated art, image enhancement, and synthetic data, and also raised concerns about deepfakes and misinformation.

>### Milestone 16 (2017): The Transformer architecture

**What happened:** Researchers published "Attention Is All You Need," introducing the Transformer architecture built on an attention mechanism.

**Why it matters:** It let models weigh the most relevant words in a sentence, making them far better at understanding context, and it became the foundation of every major language model that followed.

**Subfield:** LLMs / Deep Learning

**Impact:** Within five years it powered assistants that draft emails, write code, translate languages, and summarise documents, reshaping knowledge work across industries.

>### Milestone 17 (2022): ChatGPT brings generative AI to the mainstream

**What happened:** ChatGPT's release put a conversational large language model in front of the general public, and rivals like Claude and Gemini soon followed.

**Why it matters:** It made advanced AI usable by anyone with plain language, with no technical skill required, and it reached huge audiences within weeks.

**Subfield:** Generative AI / LLMs

**Impact:** Students, developers, and businesses adopted AI tools for writing, coding, translating, and summarising, sparking new debates about jobs, education, and regulation.

>### Milestone 18 (Future): AGI and Superintelligence

**What happened:** Artificial General Intelligence, meaning AI that matches human ability across any task, and Superintelligence, which would exceed it, remain theoretical goals rather than achievements.

**Why it matters:** Whether and how these are reached raises the biggest questions about safety, control, and society's future.

**Subfield:** AGI / Superintelligence (AI's open future)

**Impact:** Expectations about AGI already shape research funding, safety work, and government policy, even though no one knows if or when it will arrive.
